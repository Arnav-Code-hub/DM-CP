The mathematics

Warshall: can we get there?

Uses Boolean OR and AND to compute transitive closure. At stage k, a path from i to j exists if it already existed, or if i can reach k and k can reach j.

The initial matrix records edge presence, including zero-weight roads. Diagonal entries are 1: a vertex reaches itself by a zero-length path. This is reflexive transitive closure.

Floyd–Warshall: what is the shortest route?

Uses minimum and addition. At stage k, compare the previous distance with a route through k. A next-hop matrix reconstructs the route.

∞ means unreachable. A negative diagonal detects a negative cycle. Pairs that can travel through such a cycle have no finite shortest distance; their routes are suppressed.

Each stage reads the previous stage’s matrix. After stage k, paths may use vertices A through k as intermediates. Both algorithms take O(V³) time and O(V²) working space. Saving all V³ evaluations for playback takes an additional O(V³) space.

Road closures and distance changes clear old calculations. Reset rewinds playback; Load city example restores the original network. Graph labels correspond to matrix row and column labels.
